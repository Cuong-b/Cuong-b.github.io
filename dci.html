A Little Background on Drum Corps International

Drum Corps International (DCI) is considered the premier competitive marching band organization in the United States. DCI has three main competitive divisions or classes - World Class, Open Class, and All Ages - where World Class represents the highest level of competition. In the World Class division, it features 23 corps (marching band teams) that compete in a series of competitions across the country during the summer months between late May and early August. Each corps consists of 160 performing members ages 16-22 who live with the corps throughout the entire three month season. The competitions are known for their high level of musical and visual performance, and they attract thousands of spectators each year. DCI has a rich history dating back to the 1970s and has played a significant role in the development of marching band culture in the United States.

A typical DCI season consists of two main phases: Spring Training and the Summer Tour. During Spring Training, marching members spend a grueling 3-4 weeks learning and perfecting their show, which involve intense 9am to 10pm rehearsals 7 days out of the week. The corps then embarks on a Summer Tour, traveling to different cities across the country to compete in large stadium competitions against rival corps. Each competition is judged by a panel of experts who evaluate the corps' performance based on criteria such as musicality, visual presentation, and overall execution. At the end of the Summer Tour is the DCI World Championships, a three-day elimination event where the top scoring corps on the final night is crowned the DCI World Champion. The DCI season is a highly competitive and demanding experience. So much so that most corps strive to even make it as a top 12 finalists.

My DCI Experience

Over my drum corps career, I was a member of the Bluecoats Drum and Bugle Corps and Pacific Crest Drum and Bugle Corps, where I performed as part of the colorguard. My experience in DCI has been incredibly rewarding, as it has allowed me to develop my skills as a performer, work collaboratively with others, and travel to different parts of the country. It has also taught me valuable lessons about discipline, perseverance, teamwork, and grit that I still carry with me into other areas of my life.

Below I share the chronology of the DCI journey:

Pacific Crest 2019: Everglow (14th Place)

Pacific Crest 2019 was my first year in DCI, and I was both excited and nervous about the experience. At 17 years old, this was my first time being away and traveling independently from my family for an extended period of time. New to DCI, it felt like a steep uphill climb to learn the ropes and adapt to the rigorous schedule and expectations of the corps. This was definltely the year of learning but also new found freedom.

2019's show was titled Everglow. The show was about the emotions and energy surrounding profound experiences and memories that impact our lives.

Pacific Crest 2021: eX (Exhibition)

Pacific Crest 2021 was a unique year for DCI since the COVID pandemic had caused the cancellation of the 2020 season. The corps had to adapt to new safety protocols and restrictions. As a result of COVID recovery efforts, DCI decided to hold an exhibition year instead of a competitive season. Despite these challenges, I was grateful to be able to perform and connect with my fellow members in a safe and meaningful way.

2021's show was titled eX. The show was about the eXperimention, eXpression, and eXperience involved in innovation and art.

Bluecoats 2022: Riffs & Revelations (2nd Place)

Bluecoats 2022 was my first year making it big with a competitive corps. The Bluecoats won their first DCI World Championship in 2016 and have been consitently in the top 5 since 2013. Their 2016 show, Down Side Up, is widely regarded as one of the most iconic shows in DCI and was also my introduction to DCI. Since 2016, I knew the Bluecoats were the dream corps I wanted to be a part of. The Bluecoats audition process was rigorous and competitive. For the colorguard section, there were only 40 spots for more than 500 auditionees. I still remember the elation I felt when I receive the acceptance letter.

The 2022 season was by far the most challenging experience of my DCI career. It was my first experience with a highly competitive corps and the intensity of the rehearsals and performances was unlike anything I had experienced before. The corps was constantly pushing us to improve and refine our skills, and the pressure to perform at a high level was immense. Despite the challenges, I felt a strong sense of camaraderie and support from my fellow members, which helped me persevere through the tough times. At the Bluecoats, I learned how to balance discipline and playfulness, and how to push myself to achieve my full potential.

2022's show was titled Riffs & Revelations. The show was inspired by a Brad Mehldau's Taming the Dragon, a narrative jazz piece about a trippy dream whose character represent the subconscious mind. The show was about the journey of self-discovery through dreams.

Bluecoats 2024: Change is Everything (1st Place - World Champions)

Bluecoats 2024 was the holy grail of my DCI journey and the best age-out season I could ever ask for. The season was intense and the pressure was high but, with the years of drum corps I've experieced, this season was one I felt most prepared for. Not only did we win the championship, this was the Bluecoats first ever undeafeated season! The sense of accomplishment and pride that came with winning the DCI World Championship was indescribable. It was a moment that I will never forget, and it reaffirmed my love for the activity and the community that surrounds it. This final season was truly special; a culmination of all the hard work, dedication, and passion that I had put into my marching career.

2024's show was titled Change is Everything. The show was about Entropy and the inevitability of change from order to disorder. This show was made in partnership with Son Lux, who composed the soundtrack for Everything Everywhere All at Once and Thunderbolts.
