Abstract

Neutrinoless double beta decay (0νββ) is a proposed radioactive decay process that could prove the neutrino’s Majorana nature and demonstrate the violation of lepton number conservation. The observation of 0νββ would provide a pow- erful hint to the origin of the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the Universe. Amongst the next generation of 0νββ experiments, CUPID (CUORE Upgrade with Particle IDentification) will search for this process in the isotope 100Mo. One drawback to using 100Mo in this upcoming experiment is its unusually fast two neutrino double beta (2νββ) decay rate (a half-life of 7.1 × 1018 years). Due to the slow response time of the detector, two events that occur close enough in time could result in a total sum of energy that leaks into the energy region of interest. This family of events, called “pileups”, constitutes one of the major sources of background in the region of interest for the CUPID experiment. The pileup of 2νββ is expected to be the dominant contribution. Deep learning algo- rithms trained to identify these anomalies in a time series analysis can provide a major improvement to standard analysis methods. I present an analysis of the efficacy of convolutional neural networks (CNN), a deep learning architecture typically used for image recognition and classification, as a method of identifying and discriminating pileup events in time series signal data.
